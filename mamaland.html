Growing a Parent Learning Community

The Growth Opportunity

For many parents, choosing a program starts with another parent

For many parents, choosing an extracurricular program doesn't start with search. It starts with another parent:

"Where does your child take swimming lessons?"

"Do you know a good summer camp?"

"Is this class worth it?"

MamaLand helps Asian families in California discover local extracurricular activities and educational programs for their children. But while parents were already sharing recommendations with each other offline, that behavior wasn't yet built into the product experience.

That led to my core growth question:

How might we turn trusted parent recommendations into new-user acquisition?

Instead of treating referral as a separate marketing feature, I redesigned it as part of the core product journey.

From a Linear Funnel to a Growth Loop

Reframing the journey around two users

The original experience focused primarily on helping one parent discover activities. I reframed the journey around two users:

Existing parent

Finds something valuable and wants to recommend it.

New parent

Receives the recommendation and needs to quickly understand why MamaLand is useful.

This created a repeatable loop:

Discover → Find value → Share → Join → Discover

The key insight was simple:

A referral only works if both sides of the experience work.

It wasn't enough to make sharing easier. The invited parent also needed a clear path from recommendation → signup → first value.

01

Make sharing feel natural

A generic "Invite Friends" CTA asks users to promote the product. That's not how parents naturally recommend things. Parents are more likely to share when they have already found something useful for their family.

So I explored referral moments around high-intent behaviors — such as discovering an activity or finding a program another family might be interested in.

The experience shifted:

From "Invite your friends to MamaLand."
To "Know another family who would like this?"

This made referral feel like helping another parent, not helping the company acquire users.

02

Preserve the context behind the invitation

Getting someone to tap an invitation isn't the same as acquiring a user. The invited parent still needs to understand:

  • Why was I invited?
  • What can I find here?
  • Why should I sign up?

I redesigned the onboarding journey so the referral context carried into the new-user experience. Instead of sending invited parents into a generic registration flow, the experience connected the invitation with the value of MamaLand and reduced unnecessary friction before discovery.

The goal was to preserve the motivation that brought them into the product.

Referral → Lightweight onboarding → Relevant discovery

03

Get new parents to value faster

Acquisition wasn't the end goal. A successful referral needed to create another active parent, not just another account. So I looked at onboarding through the lens of time to value.

Instead of front-loading setup, I prioritized the information needed to help parents reach relevant extracurricular programs faster.

The journey shifted:

From Signup → Setup → More setup → Explore
To Join → Tell us what matters → See relevant activities

Once a new parent finds something useful, they also have a reason to share it with another family. That closes the loop.

Designing Both Sides of Growth

Treating referral as a two-sided product experience

One of the most important decisions was treating referral as a two-sided product experience.

For the sender

"Why should I share this now?"

Contextual entry points, low effort, and a clear reason to recommend.

For the recipient

"Why should I join?"

Trust, invitation context, and getting to relevant activities quickly.

Together, these two journeys transformed referral from an isolated feature into a connected growth system:

Existing-user value → Recommendation → New-user activation

Closing the Growth Loop

Connecting acquisition directly to the product experience

The redesign connected MamaLand's acquisition strategy directly to the product experience.

A parent discovers something valuable

↓ Shares it with another family

↓ That parent joins MamaLand

↓ Discovers relevant programs for their child

↓ Finds something worth recommending

↓ The loop repeats

Instead of relying only on external acquisition, the product could use existing parent value to create the next user.

Impact

A community-driven acquisition engine

For parents

  • Easier ways to recommend relevant programs to other families
  • Less friction when joining through an invitation
  • Faster access to relevant extracurricular activities
  • A more community-driven discovery experience

For the product

  • Connected referral with onboarding and activation
  • Turned existing parent behavior into a repeatable acquisition mechanism
  • Created a stronger foundation for product-led growth

What I Learned

Amplify existing behavior — don't just add entry points

Growth design isn't just about adding more entry points or making signup shorter. The more important question is:

What existing user behavior can the product amplify?

For MamaLand, parents were already helping other parents make decisions. My role was to turn that trusted behavior into a product loop — connecting discovery, recommendation, onboarding, and activation so that one successful parent experience could help create the next.
